Laptop buying guide

Get these five right and the rest follows.

Most buyer's remorse comes from one of these five decisions. Spend two minutes here before you spend money.

Storage type

Insist on an SSD. It is the single biggest difference in how fast a laptop feels day to day — bigger than the processor for ordinary work.

RAM

8GB is the practical minimum today. 16GB if you keep twenty browser tabs open, run design tools or use virtual machines.

Screen

Full HD is worth insisting on. Lower-resolution panels look soft and show less of your document or spreadsheet.

Battery & weight

If you move around Port Harcourt with it all day, weight and battery matter more than raw performance.

A word on processors: the generation matters as much as the number. A newer Core i3 or Ryzen 3 can outrun an older i5. Ask us which generation a specific unit is before comparing on the badge alone.
Work & study

For students, offices and small businesses.

Most people need the same short list: reliable Office and browser performance, a comfortable keyboard, a decent webcam for online meetings, and a battery that survives a day of lectures or client visits.

  • Students — Lenovo IdeaPad or HP Pavilion cover coursework, research and streaming.
  • Office and admin — Dell Inspiron and HP Pavilion handle Office, email and video calls all day.
  • Business and professional — HP ProBook adds a stronger chassis and business security features.
  • Creative and mobile — MacBook Air for silent operation and exceptional battery life.

Performance

What "fast" really means.

Speed is felt in three places: how quickly the machine wakes and opens apps, how many things it holds at once, and how it behaves when it gets warm.

  • SSD decides boot and app-launch speed — non-negotiable.
  • RAM decides how much you can keep open at once.
  • Cooling decides whether performance holds up after an hour of work.
  • Processor generation decides efficiency and battery life as much as raw speed.
Portability

The laptop you carry beats the one you leave at home.

A 15-inch screen is lovely on a desk and heavy in a backpack. Be honest about how often the machine travels before choosing size.

  • 13–14 inch — best for daily commuting, lectures and client meetings.
  • 15–16 inch — best if it mostly lives on a desk and you want screen space.
  • Charger weight counts — USB-C charging means one smaller adapter for everything.
  • Add a hub — slim laptops need HDMI and USB-A back; we stock the adapters.
